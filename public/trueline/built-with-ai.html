Building TrueLine with AI

By Adam Zhu

The problem

I bowled in high school and never knew why a shot missed. Lane tracking systems cost bowling centers $10,000 or more, so I built TrueLine, an iPhone app that tracks the ball from one video and reports launch speed, breakpoint and entry angle.

What I built and what the AI did

I wrote most of the code with Claude Code. I chose the approach, labeled the data, decided what to measure and tested every piece. The AI wrote the first version of most functions and ported my Python prototype to Swift.

What broke

Calibration. The first design had the user drag 4 handles onto the lane corners. It looked right in the app and the code passed its tests, but the numbers were off. I ran Monte Carlo simulations on real footage, 2,000 trials per setup, and the median error was about 3 boards. I redesigned it around 6 taps on landmarks that are easy to see, with a least-squares fit, and the simulated error dropped to about 0.5 to 0.7 boards.

Later a benchmark against 223 hand-labeled ball positions showed the tracker was lagging the ball. Fixing that brought entry board within about 0.43 boards of the hand labels, down from 2.13.

The result

TrueLine is on the App Store. I also keep a clip-by-clip test harness that runs the same videos through the Python and Swift versions and checks that they agree.

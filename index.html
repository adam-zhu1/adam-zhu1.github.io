Adam Zhu

Adam Zhu is an undergraduate at Carnegie Mellon University studying Statistics and Machine Learning. His work includes forensic image research at CSAFE, statistical analysis of VR evacuation studies, a computer-vision side project, and a published paper on matched binary diagnostic data.

Selected work

  • CSAFE, Iowa State — forensic analysis on 24,000+ camera images for device fingerprinting and handwriting similarity datasets; co-author on work under review in forensic sciences.
  • VR Applications Center (VRAC), Iowa State — statistical analysis of automated communication strategies in simulated school-shooting evacuations; co-author on a manuscript in preparation.
  • COR Robotics — co-designed curriculum and taught hands-on robotics (drones, engineering) to 100+ students in grades 3–8.
  • Team Neutrino #3928 (FIRST) — co-captain coordinating seven sub-teams and 40+ members; multiple NASA Engineering Inspiration Awards and Worlds qualifications.
  • Publication — "Statistical tests for proportion difference in one-to-two matched binary diagnostic data," Mathematics 2024, 12, 741. Read the paper.
